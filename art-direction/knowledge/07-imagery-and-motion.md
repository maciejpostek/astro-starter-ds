# Imagery And Motion

Status: universal knowledge.

Imagery, illustration, iconography, and motion extend the same composition
grammar used by typography and UI components.

## Imagery

Define:

- subject matter;
- framing and crop;
- camera or rendering style;
- color treatment;
- lighting;
- background behavior;
- aspect-ratio families;
- relationship to grid;
- accessibility and rights requirements.

An image reference must record whether the approved trait is its composition,
tone, crop, lighting, material, or subject. Do not treat an entire image as one
undifferentiated instruction.

## Illustration

Define:

- primitive shapes;
- stroke and fill behavior;
- detail level;
- perspective;
- texture;
- use of typography or symbols;
- animation potential;
- permitted semantic roles.

Do not add illustration only to fill empty space. It should reinforce content,
brand character, or product understanding.

## Iconography

Specify:

- icon source;
- stroke or fill family;
- optical size;
- corner and cap behavior;
- alignment;
- when text labels remain required;
- rules for custom brand icons.

## Motion

Motion should express the brand without obscuring state or interaction.

Define:

- tempo;
- easing character;
- continuity versus stepped behavior;
- distance and scale limits;
- hierarchy of motion;
- reduced-motion transformation;
- components allowed to animate;
- forbidden decorative motion.

## Motion And Visual Metaphor

Translate metaphor into observable behavior.

Example:

```text
modular
→ stepped reveal
→ discrete panel movement
→ shared timing intervals
→ no elastic decorative bounce
```

This example is not a starter default. A project contract must approve the
relationship.

## Evidence

Provide still references for visual treatment and short recordings or
prototypes for timing. A motion adjective without a timing example is
insufficient.
