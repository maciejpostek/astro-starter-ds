# Visual Quality Scorecard

Status: template.

This scorecard supports human review. It does not replace taste, judgment, or
approval.

Use:

```text
0 = contradicted or absent
1 = weak
2 = partially convincing
3 = clear and consistent
4 = distinctive, coherent, and production-ready
N/A = not applicable
```

## Hierarchy

- The primary, secondary, and tertiary levels are immediately legible.
- Visual emphasis matches semantic importance.
- Long and short content preserve the hierarchy.

## Contrast

- Differences communicate role, state, or emphasis.
- Accent and high-contrast treatments remain controlled.
- Accessibility and visual hierarchy both work.

## Balance

- Visual weight is intentionally distributed.
- Dense and empty areas support one another.
- Asymmetry, when present, remains anchored.

## Rhythm

- Repeated spacing, dimensions, and alignments create a coherent cadence.
- Interruptions correspond to meaningful emphasis.
- Component and page rhythms agree.

## Unity

- Components share recognisable DNA without becoming identical.
- Typography, color, geometry, and interaction rules reinforce one another.
- The system remains coherent when brand imagery is removed.

## Brand Expression

- Approved project principles are observable.
- The result cannot be explained only as generic good UI.
- Reference traits are interpreted rather than copied.

## Composition

- Grid roles and alignment are visible.
- Negative space has a clear role.
- Responsive states preserve the creative thesis.

## Component Integrity

- Visual changes preserve the real UX and accessibility contract.
- Variants represent stable roles rather than aesthetic alternatives.
- Token use does not hide component-local optical problems.

## Overall Review

Record:

- strongest quality;
- weakest quality;
- unresolved tension;
- approved propagation scope;
- reviewer;
- date;
- decision: `reject | revise | approve-pilot | approve-family | approve-system`.
