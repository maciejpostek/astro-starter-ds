## UX purpose

Hero5050 opens a page with one primary promise, concise supporting evidence and an equally prominent crop-safe visual. It keeps the main content compact while reserving a separate lower action area for a caption and related calls to action.

## Communication role

- Goals: introduction

Introduce the page proposition and audience relevance. Use the approved hierarchy and existing visual design; do not redefine the hero pattern per page.

## Use when

- A page needs one leading hero whose copy and visual carry equal weight.
- The opening promise benefits from optional BulletPoint evidence and a short action-area caption.
- The visual should fill one half of the wide layout and remain meaningful after cropping.

## Avoid when

- The page title is owned by another component or the pattern is not the first main section.
- The copy is long-form, the visual must never crop or the layout needs a form, search field or background image treatment.
- The content needs the deliberately separated heading/details composition owned by HeroSpaced5050.

## Choose instead

- `HeroSpaced5050` — when the heading and supporting details need a spacious separated rhythm without bullets or a caption.
- `HeroBreakout` — when inset breakout media is the approved design and an Astro implementation becomes available.
- `HeroFullVisual` — when media should span the full section and an Astro implementation becomes available.
- `Feature5050` — for an ordinary lower-page feature section with a selectable visual side.

## Content contract

- `heading` and the `visual` slot are required. Hero5050 always renders the heading as the page-level `h1` with the fixed Heading/H4 visual style.
- `eyebrow`, `paragraph` and `caption` are optional non-empty strings. Omit absent content rather than passing empty strings.
- The optional `bulletPoints` slot accepts direct BulletPoint children. Keep each statement short and independently understandable.
- The optional `actions` slot accepts ButtonGroup-compatible actions. Prefer one primary action and at most one genuine peer action.
- Keep copy concise enough to preserve the 50–50 relationship under localization and text zoom.

## Composition and placement

- Use once as the first section inside `main`; do not nest it in another section shell that adds site padding.
- Preserve source order as Content, BulletPoint list, caption/actions and visual. Wide layout placement must not change reading or focus order.
- Content owns the eyebrow, heading and paragraph rhythm. BulletPoint owns each list item and fixed status icon. ButtonGroup owns action grouping and wrapping.
- Provide meaningful alternative text for informative media and an empty alternative for purely decorative media.

## Responsive behavior

- Primary strategy: `container`
- Mechanisms and references: `.hero-50-50.l-section`, `.l-grid[data-grid="breakout"]`, a nested six-column `.l-grid[data-grid="columns"]`, logical sizing, `100svh`, native `aspect-ratio` and approved global layout, size, color and typography tokens.
- Container queries: the named `hero-50-50` inline-size container keeps the 50–50 named-line composition at 64rem and above; below 64rem content stays inside the padded main content lines while the following visual spans `full-start / full-end` without inline padding and uses a 4:3 crop boundary.
- Viewport queries: none; `100svh` is the wide-layout minimum-height mechanic and is not a public variant.
- Reflow, order and visibility: narrow layouts keep all content before visual, optional regions disappear only through prop or slot absence, ButtonGroup retains wrapping ownership and no alternate markup is rendered.

## Accessibility and required behavior

- The root is a native `section` labelled by the required Content heading through `aria-labelledby`.
- Hero5050 owns the primary page heading and always renders one semantic `h1`; do not place it on a page that already has a different primary heading.
- The bullet region is a native list whose direct children must be BulletPoint list items.
- ButtonGroup is labelled by the hero heading; slotted controls retain their native keyboard and focus behavior.
- Reflow must preserve DOM, reading and focus order. Captions do not replace media alternative text, and media controls or motion behavior remain the caller's responsibility.

## Related components

- [Content](/design-system/website-patterns/content) owns the heading-led copy and is extended to support this top-level `h1` composition.
- [BulletPoint](/design-system/website-patterns/bullet-points/bullet-point) owns optional evidence statements and their fixed icon.
- [ButtonGroup](/design-system/base-components/buttons/button-group) owns action grouping and intrinsic wrapping.

## Naming and token contract

Use the stable `Hero5050` identity, `.hero-50-50` root and `hero` family. Consume only registered global layout, size, color and typography values plus Content, BulletPoint and ButtonGroup contracts. Do not declare component custom properties, `.ds-*` classes, arbitrary layout props, raw colors, fixed Figma widths or Figma grid-measurement Variables.

## Brand Expression boundary

The semantic canvas and surface colors, typography and approved spacing aliases may change through an approved Brand Expression Contract. The `h1` ownership, source order, dependency boundaries, equal wide allocation, required visual and narrow reflow remain stable component invariants.

## Figma–Astro differences

Astro omits the structural `Type=Default` property, derives optional visibility from content presence, flattens nested Content decisions into the section API, requires a semantic visual slot, renders a fixed `h1`, uses `100svh` instead of the 800px presentation height and adds the accepted source-order-preserving stack below 64rem.

## Forbidden shortcuts

- Do not replace Content, BulletPoint or ButtonGroup with local markup or override their internal spacing and icons.
- Do not expose `Type`, `show*`, heading-level, visual-position, arbitrary icon or styling props.
- Do not hardcode Figma widths, add a Ratio dependency, create Hero-specific tokens or visually reorder the media before the heading.

## Core decision

Reuse Hero5050 only for a page-opening, equal-weight copy-and-visual hero that needs optional bullet evidence and a separate caption/action area. Its accepted Astro differences provide semantic HTML and responsive runtime behavior without changing the canonical Desktop visual intent.
